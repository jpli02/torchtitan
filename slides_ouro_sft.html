Ouro on Terminal-Bench

Thirteen SFT checkpoints from one 1.4B looped transformer. Where each one started, what it was trained on, what it scored — and the three experiments that finally said what the ceiling is.

Base model throughout: Ouro-1.4B-Thinking, a universal transformer that runs its weight-tied decoder up to 4 times per token behind a learned early-exit gate. Pretrained, it scores 0/80 — not for lack of ability, but because it cannot be driven: 866–1,486 tokens per turn and it never stops.

branch worktree-ouro-terminal-sft · 543e63721
Setup

One recipe, one eval, thirteen checkpoints

Training (shared by every run)

Assistant-only loss masking. Ouro's Stage-I objective, L = Σₜ p(t|x)·L⁽ᵗ⁾ − β·H(p), β = 0.01. LR 2e-5 from base, 1e-5 for continuations, 50-step warmup. Batch 1, sequence 4096, full activation checkpointing, one GPU.

Evaluation

80 local tasks (core-0.1.x format, 4 .easy variants). Agent terminus-2, temperature 0.7, no n-gram guard. Budget 420 s — trials actually run to ~720 s. A 12-task subset for fast reads, 2 attempts each.

Noise floor: ±1 task

Two same-recipe runs scored 4/80 and 3/80 and shared only 2 of their solved tasks. On 12 tasks, anything within one task is not a result.

Checkpoint 01sft1k

The first checkpoint that could be driven

The pretrained model produces ~9 usable turns across all 80 tasks: 866–1,486 tokens each, never a stop token, client timeouts everywhere. One thousand steps on the original mix fixed that — the model learned to emit parseable terminus-2 JSON and to end a turn.

Two serving bugs masked it at first. max_tokens defaulted to 1,024 and cut every response mid-reasoning (0% pass); an n-gram repeat guard corrupted JSON under sampling (pass@5 0.000). With both fixed the same weights scored pass@5 0.400 on the five simplest tasks.

SFT's benefit is controllability. Every later checkpoint inherits this; none of them gets much beyond it.

Checkpoint 02sft10k

Ten times the steps, same data

The natural follow-up: the same original mix from the same base, run to 10k steps on one GPU. Evaluated head-to-head against the pretrained model, it confirmed the pattern already visible at 1k — the agent is controllable, and controllability is most of what it gains.

It was superseded by continue10k before the 80-task protocol existed, so it does not appear in the final comparison tables.

Steps alone did not change the picture. This is the first of several signs that the objective, not the budget, was the limit.

Checkpoint 03continue10k

The best real checkpoint — and the reference for everything after

Started from sft1k, not the base, and continued for 10k steps on a mix reweighted toward longer trajectories: TerminalTraj at 60%. It solves four of 80 tasks and holds that record.

It also defined the noise floor. A same-recipe reproduction (repro11k) scored 3/80 and shared only two of its four solved tasks — fix-permissions and hello-world are reliable; the other two are a coin flip.

4/80 is the high-water mark for SFT on public corpora. Every intervention below is measured against this checkpoint.

Checkpoint 04router2k

Train only the exit gate

Ouro's early-exit router is 2,049 parameters. stage2_adaptive freezes everything else and trains just the gate, from sft1k, for 2k steps. The point was efficiency: a sharper gate exits the 4-step loop earlier per token.

It proved the latency lever — later measured at −33% to −44% compute at a flat ability score — without changing what the model can do.

Efficiency and ability are separable here. The gate is a compute dial, not a capability dial — which is what made it a clean NAS objective later.

Checkpoint 05sft20k

Twice the budget, the worst score

From the base, 20k steps on the union of the two earlier mixes. It scores 2/80 — only the two tasks every checkpoint solves — and threw 14 unknown_agent_error failures.

Held-out cross-entropy kept improving throughout. That dissociation — CE falls, task success does not move — recurs across seven data interventions: 1.292 → 0.461 → 0.391.

More steps sharpen the mode. Token-level CE learns the most common action; longer training trains minority behaviours out, not in.

Checkpoint 07litecoder+1k

Longer horizons made it worse

Training trajectories averaged 8–16 turns against a benchmark median of 22, so the obvious fix was a longer corpus. LiteCoder-Terminal-SFT averages 53 turns and is already in terminus JSON. It went in at 60%, on top of continue10k, for 1k steps.

Score fell from 0.208 to 0.125 — 2 of 12 tasks against 3 — on clean runs with zero server errors. Mean tokens per turn drifted up, 155 → 163.

Horizon was the wrong lever. The recorded conclusion at the time; the oracle experiments later explained why.

Checkpoint 08batching5k

Fix the behaviour the data had erased

continue10k emitted exactly one shell command per turn in 643 of 660 turns — never more — while its own training data averaged 1.72 with a max of 9. CE had collapsed onto the singleton mode. The fix filtered the mix to trajectories averaging ≥1.5 commands per turn (36% of rows kept, median 4.0) and continued 5k steps at a lower LR.

The behaviour came back: multi-command turns went from 0/660 to 48/406, timeouts fell 17 → 14. pass@1 did not move — until the harness gate (slide 16) turned its reclaimed time into a new solved task.

It hit its target and missed the outcome — then became the base for the best 12-task score.

Checkpoints 09–11traj1k · traj10k · traj20k

One verified corpus, from scratch

Per-corpus measurement showed Nemotron-medium abandons 88% of its trajectories and had been 30–45% of every mix — roughly a third of all training signal to date was sessions that give up. TerminalTraj is the only corpus that is 100% terminus-2 format, and its paper reports trajectories synthesized with executable validation code.

So: TerminalTraj alone, finished trajectories only (92% kept, median 15 turns), from the pretrained base. At 1k steps: 3/24. At 10k: 4/24 with partial credit at 27.8% against the mix's 45.5%. At 20k: 5/24 — tying continue10k — with partial credit at 54.3%, the highest of any real checkpoint, and the fewest timeouts of any ungated run (12).

Verified data lost at 1k and 10k, then caught the mix at 20k. Binary is a tie; the +26pp partial-credit swing between 10k and 20k of the same run is ~1.5σ over continue10k — suggestive, not decisive. First single-corpus run that is not worse than the mix.

Checkpoint 14tb80sft

Data built for the benchmark's distribution

The oracle pair said what the data had to be: verified, multi-turn, on-distribution. This set is that. 569 real agent runs on Terminal-Bench 2.0 tasks that share no name with our 80 — scraped leaderboard trajectories where the task's tests passed — re-rendered into the exact terminus-2 JSON the eval agent parses, upsampled ×5 and capped per task; plus 4,479 TerminalTraj trajectories reweighted to TB-80's category mix. 7,324 rows, ~63M tokens, one epoch and a bit at 20k steps.

Same recipe and base as traj20k, so data is the only variable. Ungated it scores 7/24 over four tasks — the best stock-agent result of the project. csv-to-parquet fell 2/2 without the gate, which until now it had only done with it; processing-pipeline had never been solved by anything. And the gate added nothing (early quits 6 → 5): the on-distribution data taught the model to finish what it can finish, the behaviour the gate was compensating for.

7/24 under stock terminus-2 is directly comparable to published numbers. One seed; the 80-task run decides it.

Checkpoint 12oracle12diagnostic · contaminated by construction

Train on the test: what is the ceiling?

If a model trained on the exact solutions still cannot execute them through the agent loop, the bottleneck is not data. Each row pairs a task's verbatim episode-0 prompt — harvested from our own eval logs, so the prompt distribution matches eval exactly — with its oracle solution rendered as one terminus-2 turn, then the real confirmation turn.

Result: 20/24, and the two misses were a harness bug (a 5-second wait on scripts that install perl or wait for a cron tick). The model emitted every solution correctly, including an 8,000-character chess program, in exactly two episodes per trial with zero timeouts.

Effectively 12/12. Execution, format, the double-confirm, real containers — all work. The ceiling is not capacity. Never a reportable score.

Do the demonstrations transfer?

Same construction, same base, same LR — but trained on the oracle solutions of the 68 tasks not in the 12-task set, and evaluated on the 12. The only variable is which tasks the demonstrations came from.

3/24. And the behaviour tells the story: 2.1 episodes per trial, 17 of 24 trials opened by writing a heredoc "solution", 20 of 24 declared task_complete on their very first turn — on tasks they had never seen, with invented contents. Partial credit fell to 25.7%, below the no-op floor.

Shape transferred; skill did not. Oracle scripts contain the answer but not the process — no exploration, observation, or recovery — and SFT on them teaches the model to skip the process.

What moved pass@1 · 1 of 2

Forty lines of harness beat seven data mixes

terminus-2 already asks "are you sure?" before grading. Our models answered by re-emitting task_complete: true with an empty commands list — 8 of 8 times at a mean 28–33% of tests passing. The gate refuses a confirmation that ran nothing, up to twice, and asks for verification instead.

On batching5k that produced the best 12-task score and a task no checkpoint had solved. It hurt continue10k (5 → 4), which mostly times out and gained nothing from being told to keep going — exactly what the mechanism predicts.

What moved pass@1 · 2 of 2 — efficiency

Router NAS through BO: Claude ≈ gp_hedge, both beat random

A 6-D search over the exit gate — depth, width, activation, input norm, jointly with adaptive_gamma and the exit threshold — each candidate trained from random init for 100 steps and scored by avg_loops subject to an accuracy floor. 25 evaluations per optimizer, ~8 minutes each.

All three best points sit at pass@1 0.80, so this is purely a compute comparison. Claude and gp_hedge tie on quality; they win differently — Claude keeps 84% of its evaluations feasible (it reads the axis names), gp_hedge reaches the target in 10 iterations to Claude's 18.

One seed: the Claude/gp_hedge ordering is noise, the gap to random is not. Both converged to layers=0, the original linear gate — the win came from gamma and threshold.

Conclusion

The ceiling is deriving solutions, not executing them

Executing a known solution

12/12
oracle12. Through the real agent loop, real containers, the double-confirm. The model, the harness, and the format are not the problem.

Learning from 68 solutions

3/24
oracle68. It learned to write a script and declare done. Answers without process teach skipping the process.

Verified synthetic trajectories

4/24
traj10k. Correctness labels at a 10k budget did not beat a messy mix a third of which gives up.

What is missing is demonstrations of the search — explore, observe, adapt, verify, then complete — on tasks of this distribution. The corpus that helps partial credit most is the exploratory one. The two untested levers are rejection-sampled distillation from a strong agent on the 68 held-out tasks, and RL on the task reward itself.

What is running, and what is waiting on a decision

tb80sft v1 is void — the test is being redone

Inspecting a rendered row exposed a build bug: yoonholee's system step is a trial's fully assembled prompt (template + task + terminal screen), and the builder cached the first row's whole prompt as "the template". So every one of the 2,845 on-distribution rows opened with a complete wrong task (adaptive-rejection-sampler) before its own, headerless. Task/trajectory overlap: 0.08 in the rendered rows vs 0.45 at the source (label check 562/562 clean — the bug was mine). The 7/24 and the clean 2/80 both came from a checkpoint trained on those rows, so the earlier "distribution-matched data didn't generalise" conclusion is retracted — it was never a valid test. Fixed and rebuilt: overlap 0.48, one header and one terminal state per prompt. v2 is retraining now (same recipe, 20k steps) with the 12-task evals chained; this is the real test.

Proposed

Rejection-sampled distillation. Run a strong agent through terminus-2 on the 68 held-out tasks, keep only verified successes, SFT from continue10k, evaluate the 12. Same clean held-out design as oracle68, but demonstrations of the search rather than the answer. Needs a strong model for ~68 agent episodes.

RL on task reward (GRPO or rejection-sampling SFT on the model's own successful rollouts) — the only method that trains on the model's own states with a success signal.
